The flagship of the Kit. Session to Song reads your Session clips and recreates
them in the Arrangement at the bars you lay out: real clips on real tracks,
named, colored, with named cue points marking each section.

## What it does

You map which Session scenes become which song sections and give each a length
in bars. Before writing, it validates references, destination ranges, source geometry, and
the complete placement plan. It then runs up to three ordered mutation phases: clear the target
range, create physical clip repetitions and cue points, then populate clip content, properties,
and cue names. MIDI notes are copied; audio clips are referenced by file.

## Right-click path

Right-click a Scene, then select **Build Arrangement from Session…**. A dialog opens with a
small table: one row per section, each with a
name, a scene picker, a bar count, and a color. A free-text field also parses a
line like `Intro 8, Verse 16, Chorus 16, Bridge 8, Outro 8`. Press Build. A
reverse **Export Structure** reads existing cue points back to text.

## Undo and recovery

The receipt reports the exact intended undo count: 0 for a no-op, 2 for a cue-only build with no
clear, or 3 when the command clears, creates, and populates. Do not describe the build as atomic.
If creation fails after clear, the typed partial error reports `undoStepsToRestore: 1`. If
population fails after clear and create, it reports `undoStepsToRestore: 2`. Follow that exact
count to restore the pre-run state, then re-list source objects, preflight again, and retry.

## Beta limit

Session to Song reads your Session clips and recreates them in the Arrangement at
the bars you lay out. It copies MIDI notes and references audio clips by file,
plus clip name and color. It does not carry over every clip nuance: warp markers,
clip envelopes, and follow actions are not part of what the beta lets an
extension write, so complex warped or automated clips may need a touch-up after
the build. It assumes 4/4 unless a scene's time signature is read (the SDK has no
Set-level time signature; signature lives on the scene). Locators are written as
cue points (time plus name), not Live's colored section locators. It is
user-invoked. The receipt reports physical clip and cue counts so the runtime result can be
checked against the plan.
